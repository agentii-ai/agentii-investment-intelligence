# Hostile Takeover Defense & Offense — Institutional Methodology

Advanced deal-process methodology synthesized from institutional M&A training; all text is original paraphrase. No basic definitions. Practitioner-grade operational knowledge only.

---

## Tender Offer Mechanics & Regulatory Timeline

The tender offer bypasses the target board by soliciting shares directly from shareholders under Williams Act regulation. It is structured as a two-step acquisition:

**Step 1**: Bidder announces a firm, non-negotiated offer at a premium to market, contingent on tendering a minimum number of shares (typically a majority). The offer must remain open at least 20 business days. Issuer must disclose its position — recommend, reject, or no opinion — within 10 business days. Payment within 3 business days of close.

**Step 2**: Acquirer eliminates remaining minority holders. Two paths: **short-form merger** (if ownership exceeds the state threshold, typically 90%, the second step executes automatically without minority solicitation) or **long-form merger** (SEC-approved proxy statement; dual-track — running tender while preparing proxy — is standard).

**Two-tier coercive structure**: Higher compensation for early tendering shareholders to accelerate surrender. May conflict with fair-price provisions in certain states prohibiting discriminatory pricing.

## Proxy Contest Process

Schedule 14A must be filed with the SEC before solicitation. The dissident files preliminary proxy materials, receives SEC review and comment, then distributes definitive materials to shareholders. The company files its own proxy or consent revocation statement simultaneously. ISS and Glass Lewis issue voting recommendations that heavily influence institutional voters.

**Combined proxy-tender strategy**: A proxy fight installs board nominees who then redeem the pill and dismantle defenses, clearing the path for a tender offer. This sequenced approach is standard against classified boards.

**Consent solicitation variant**: Dissidents solicit written consents to remove directors or amend bylaws without waiting for the annual meeting. The Sanofi-Medivation contest (2016) saw both sides distribute competing color-coded consent/revocation cards.

## Poison Pill Architecture

Modern pills are structured as rights offerings (call options) operating on a dual-trigger system:

**First trigger**: Upon bid announcement, rights detach from shares. Exercise price is set at 3-5x market price, calibrated by formal valuation to reflect long-term intrinsic value, not current trading.

**Second trigger**: Upon threshold crossing (10-25% of outstanding), rights become exercisable. Non-acquirer shareholders purchase shares at 50% discount, giving each right a value of 2x its exercise price.

**Flip-in**: Right to buy target stock at discount, diluting the acquirer's economic and voting stake. **Flip-over**: Right to buy acquirer's stock at discount post-merger.

**Dilution math (Netflix 2012)**: $70 stock, $350 exercise, 55M shares, 20% trigger. Upon activation: each right buys shares worth $700 for $350. Total new shares issued = 440M. Post-issuance count = 495M, new price = $38.88. Activist ownership collapses from 20% to 2.2% (44% value loss).

**Pill variants**: Dead-hand (only adopting board can redeem), slow-hand (deferred redemption period), NOL pill (5% position cap to protect net operating losses from Section 382 limitations). Any board can adopt a pill "in play" within 24-48 hours without shareholder vote.

## Active & Preventive Defense Arsenal

**White Knight / Squire**: A friendly bidder or blocking minority investor. Pfizer intervened against Sanofi's pursuit of Medivation. **Pac-Man defense**: Target counterbids for the aggressor — Jos. A. Bank bid for Men's Wearhouse after Men's Wearhouse launched first.

**Standstill agreements**: Acquirer agrees not to increase ownership. The "Don't Ask / Don't Waive" variant (Transatlantic/Validus/Allied World) prevents even private waiver requests.

**Staggered board**: Directors divided into three classes with one-third elected annually. Even after gaining majority control, hostile acquirer must win two shareholder meetings to control the board. Combined with a poison pill, this is the strongest standard defense.

**Supermajority requirements**: Merger approval requires 2/3 to 80% of shareholder votes. **Fair price provisions**: Block discriminatory or coercive offer structures.

**Customer assurance programs**: PeopleSoft against Oracle (2003) — rebates up to 5x software purchase price triggered on change of control, creating a contingent liability that made the bid economically non-viable.

**Severance triggers**: Microsoft's bid for Yahoo (2008) carried $462M-$2.72B in change-of-control severance, functioning as a de facto cost barrier.

**Leveraged recapitalization**: Target takes on significant debt for a dividend or buyback, consuming cash and increasing leverage to deter acquirers (Owens Corning).

## Board Fiduciary Duty: Three Delaware Gatekeepers

**Unocal Standard**: Defensive measures are valid if (1) reasonable grounds exist to believe the bid threatens corporate policy, and (2) the defense is reasonable relative to the threat. The board must demonstrate informed business judgment.

**Revlon Duty**: Once change of control is inevitable, the board shifts from defender to auctioneer — it must maximize shareholder value. Revlon's board violated this by granting Forstmann-Little a lock-up option on the vision care unit below fair value to block Perelman.

**Time-Warner Standard**: When pursuing a long-term strategic plan, the board may reject a higher immediate premium if the strategic transaction serves long-term shareholder interests. Directors need not always follow majority shareholder preference.

## Bear Hug Architecture & 13D/G Strategy

The Anthem-Cigna bear hug letter (June 2015) reveals the standard architecture: (1) strategic fit framing, (2) specific price with premium calculation (35.4% over unaffected), (3) consideration mix (31.4% stock / 68.6% cash), (4) pro forma ownership split (76.3% / 23.7%), (5) EPS accretion (10%+ Year 1, doubling Year 2), (6) synergy and financing commitment, (7) regulatory confidence, (8) negotiation history documenting prior proposals and governance disputes, (9) CEO succession and board composition terms, and (10) explicit threat to approach shareholders directly if rebuffed.

**Filing strategy**: Schedule 13D within 10 days of acquiring 5% if control intent exists. Schedule 13G for passive institutions — notably triggers a higher pill threshold (20% vs 10% for 13D filers in the Netflix pill), penalizing activist intent while accommodating passive ownership.
