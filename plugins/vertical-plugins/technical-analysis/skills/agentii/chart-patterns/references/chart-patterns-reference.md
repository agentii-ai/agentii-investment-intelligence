## The Setup Architecture

### Setup = Context + Signal Bar

A trading setup consists of exactly two components:
1. **Context**: The current phase of the market cycle (trend direction, strength, position within the cycle)
2. **Signal Bar**: The specific candlestick pattern that triggers entry

**Neither component alone is sufficient.** A perfect signal bar in the wrong context is a losing trade. A correct context without a signal bar is not a trade — it's a guess.

### Signal Bar vs Entry Bar
- **Signal Bar**: The bar *before* you enter. It creates the signal.
- **Entry Bar**: The bar when you actually enter the trade.
- **Best Signal Bars**: Reversal bars — bars that close opposite to their direction after piercing an extreme.

### The Golden Rule: Context Dictates Everything

In a **strong bear trend**: Sell For Any Reason, Including Below Bull Bars. Do Not Buy, Even If Strong Buy Candlestick Pattern.

In a **strong bull trend**: Buy For Any Reason, Including Above Bear Bars. Do Not Sell, Even If Strong Bear Reversal Bar.

This is the most important concept in pattern recognition: the trend context overrides any individual candlestick pattern. A perfect bearish engulfing at the top of a strong bull trend is a **buy signal** because it will likely fail and become a bull flag.

## Pullbacks and Bar Counting

### The H1/H2/H3/H4 Counting System

When the market pulls back in a trend, each time a bar closes beyond the prior bar's extreme, it ends one leg of the pullback. Count these legs to time your entry:

| Setup | Legs | Market Condition | Entry Rule |
|-------|:---:|-----------------|-----------|
| **H1 / L1** | 1-leg pullback | Strong Trend | Enter immediately. Strong trends rarely give deep pullbacks. |
| **H2 / L2** | 2-leg pullback | Channel / Moderate Trend | Most common setup. Wait for second leg to complete. |
| **H3 / L3** | 3-leg pullback | Wider Channel / Weaker Trend | Wait for third leg. More cautious entry. |
| **H4 / L4** | 4-leg pullback | Trading Range near Trend | Highest probability but rarest. Wait for fourth leg. |

**Naming**: H2 = High 2 = second push up in a bull trend (second leg of a pullback that creates a higher low). L2 = Low 2 = second push down in a bear trend.

**Entry**: Place buy stop 1 tick above the high of the signal bar (for H1/H2/H3/H4 in a bull trend). Place sell stop 1 tick below the low of the signal bar (for L1/L2/L3/L4 in a bear trend).

**Stop**: 1 tick beyond the opposite extreme of the signal bar.

**The edge**: After two failed attempts to resume the trend, both bulls and bears are forced to act in the same direction. This behavioral fact is what gives the H2/L2 its statistical edge.

## Channels

### Channel Classification

A channel is a weak trend contained between two parallel lines. Despite being "weak," channels can still be very steep and strong in appearance.

| Channel Type | Structure | Characteristics |
|-------------|----------|----------------|
| **Bull Channel** | Rising trend line below, channel line above | Higher highs and lows. Contained between two upward-sloping lines. |
| **Bear Channel** | Falling trend line above, channel line below | Lower highs and lows. Contained between two downward-sloping lines. |
| **Tight Channel** | Narrow parallel lines, bars barely overlap | Strong for a channel. Pullbacks are small or nonexistent. |
| **Broad Channel** | Wide parallel lines, significant bar overlap | Weak for a channel. More two-sided trading within. |

### Channel Evolution
Channels inevitably get flatter and broader over time, eventually becoming trading ranges. This is a law of market structure.

### Micro Channels
A micro channel is an extremely tight series of 5-10 consecutive trend bars with minimal overlap. Properties:
- Represents extreme, one-sided pressure
- Breakouts from micro channels are more reliable than from regular channels
- The first pullback after a micro channel break is a high-probability entry

## Trading Ranges and Vacuums

### Trading Range Properties
- A sideways market where both bulls and bears are active
- Breakout can be in either direction
- Trade boundaries: buy near low, sell near high
- Middle of the range is no-man's land — avoid trading

### Vacuum
A vacuum is an area where price moved through quickly with little to no trading (a gap zone). Key properties:
- Price tends to return to fill vacuums
- After a vacuum fill, the original direction often resumes
- Vacuums act as magnets for price

## Breakouts and Gaps

### True vs False Breakouts
A breakout occurs when price moves beyond the boundary of a trading range or channel. The key skill is distinguishing true breakouts from false ones.

**Strong Breakout Characteristics**:
- Multiple consecutive big bars in the breakout direction
- Small tails on breakout bars
- Little to no overlap between bars
- Follow-through on subsequent bars (no immediate reversal)

**Weak / False Breakout Characteristics**:
- Single bar breaks out, then immediately reverses
- Breakout bar has a large tail
- Price returns inside the prior range within 1-3 bars
- Breakout occurs late in a trend (exhaustion gap)

### Gap Types
1. **Breakaway Gap**: Initiates a new trend, breaks away from consolidation
2. **Measuring Gap** (Runaway Gap): Mid-trend, used to project measured move target
3. **Exhaustion Gap**: Near trend end, signals potential reversal

## Trend Types

| Trend Type | Bar Characteristics | Trading Strategy |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Strong Trend** | Big bodies, small tails, minimal overlap, consecutive | Buy (bull) / Sell (bear) at market. Do not fade. |
| **Weak Trend (Channel)** | Bars with tails, overlap, contained between lines | Buy pullbacks (bull) / Sell rallies (bear) near trend line |
| **Tight Channel** | Narrow, bars barely overlapping | Treat as strong trend — enter immediately |
| **Broad Channel** | Wide, significant two-sided trading | Fade the channel boundaries |

## Reversals

### The 5-Step Reversal Sequence

All trend reversals follow the same mechanical 5-step process. This is the most important framework for identifying genuine reversals versus failed attempts:

1. **Pullback breaks the trend line** — The first signal. Price violates the channel boundary.
2. **Pullback tests the moving average** — Price returns to interact with the 20-bar EMA.
3. **Trend resumes** — The original trend attempts to continue (this is the trap for trend followers).
4. **Near old trend extreme, another pullback begins** — Price approaches but fails to surpass the prior extreme. Selling/buying pressure initiates a new counter-move.
5. **This pullback grows into reversal** — Unlike prior pullbacks, this one expands in magnitude and duration, confirming trend change.

**Key insight**: Step 3 (trend resumption) is critical. It creates the false hope that the trend is continuing, setting up the actual reversal. Many traders enter on Step 3 and get trapped.

### Most Reversal Patterns = Trading Ranges
Most classic reversal patterns (double tops, H&S, wedges) are simply trading ranges at the end of a trend. They function as transition zones where the market decides whether to continue or reverse.

---

## Wedges

A wedge is a three-push pattern at a trend extreme, indicating exhaustion:
- **Wedge Top**: Three pushes up in a bull trend → bearish reversal signal. Each push is weaker than the last.
- **Wedge Bottom**: Three pushes down in a bear trend → bullish reversal signal.
- Wedges often coincide with and confirm the 5-step reversal sequence.

---

## Double Tops and Bottoms

Double tops/bottoms are reversal patterns that also function as final flags:
- **Double Top**: Two pushes up to approximately the same level → potential bearish reversal
- **Double Bottom**: Two pushes down to approximately the same level → potential bullish reversal
- Most double tops/bottoms are actually trading ranges. The key is whether price breaks the neckline (the low between the two tops, or the high between the two bottoms).

## Triangles

| Triangle Type | Direction Bias | Notes |
|--------------|:---:|------|
| **Symmetrical Triangle** | None (50/50) | Wait for breakout direction. Do not anticipate. |
| **Ascending Triangle** | Bullish | Higher lows, flat top. Breakout likely upward. |
| **Descending Triangle** | Bearish | Lower highs, flat bottom. Breakout likely downward. |

## Head and Shoulders

The Head and Shoulders pattern is a specific type of Major Trend Reversal:
- **Left Shoulder**: First push up, then pullback
- **Head**: Higher push up (above left shoulder), then deeper pullback
- **Right Shoulder**: Lower push up (below head), then pullback below neckline
- **Validation**: Right shoulder must be lower than the left shoulder. Break below the neckline confirms.
- In a strong bull trend, most H&S patterns **fail** and become bull flags.

## Rounding Tops and Bottoms

A gradual, arcing transition from trend to opposite trend. Common misconceptions:
- NOT a single-bar reversal — it's a gradual process
- The slope of the 20-bar EMA gradually changes direction
- Often preceded by a climax that fails to produce an immediate reversal

---

## Climaxes

### Buy Climax
- Consecutive strong bull bars at the end of an up-move
- Suddenly interrupted by a large bear bar closing near its low
- The market has "exhausted" buyers
- After a climax, the market usually enters a **trading range**, NOT an immediate reversal

### Sell Climax
- Consecutive strong bear bars at the end of a down-move
- Suddenly interrupted by a large bull bar closing near its high
- The market has "exhausted" sellers

### Critical Rule: Climax → Trading Range, NOT → Reversal
The most common mistake is trading a climax as an immediate reversal. After a climax, price typically enters a trading range for many bars before any reversal. Trade the range, not the anticipated reversal.

---

## Final Flags

A final flag is the last flag pattern before a trend reversal:
- Appears as a small consolidation at the trend extreme
- Breaks out briefly in the trend direction (trapping late trend-followers)
- Then reverses sharply
- Often coincides with a climax

## Measured Moves, Support, and Resistance

### Measured Move Projection
The measured move is based on the concept that market moves tend to be symmetrical:
1. Measure the height of the first leg (from start to first pullback)
2. Project that same height from the end of the pullback
3. This gives the measured move target

### Support and Resistance
- **Support**: A prior swing low where buyers previously entered. Defined by the lowest point of the most recent pullback.
- **Resistance**: A prior swing high where sellers previously entered.
- Support and resistance are zones, not exact prices. Give 1-2 ticks of leeway.
- The more times a level is tested, the weaker it becomes.

## Pattern Summary: When to Use Each

| Market State | Valid Patterns | Invalid Patterns |
|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Strong Bull Trend | H1, H2 pullbacks; breakouts above swing highs | Any short/sell pattern (even "perfect" ones) |
| Strong Bear Trend | L1, L2 pullbacks; breakouts below swing lows | Any long/buy pattern |
| Bull Channel | H2, H3 near channel bottom; fade near channel top | H1 (not enough reward-to-risk in channels) |
| Bear Channel | L2, L3 near channel top; fade near channel bottom | L1 |
| Trading Range (small) | Buy low boundary, sell high boundary | Middle of range |
| Reversal Attempt | Wait for 5-step sequence to complete | Early entry before Step 5 confirms |
| Climax | Trade the range, not the anticipated reversal | Immediate counter-trend entry |
