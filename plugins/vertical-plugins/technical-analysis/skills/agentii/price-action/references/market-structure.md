## Price Action: The Bedrock

Price Action is any representation of price movement on any financial instrument, any chart type, and any timeframe. Every tick, every bar, every pattern is price action. The market records the collective decisions of every participant continuously — it is the most complete information source available.

**Core Principle**: Price action is genetically based. It reflects human behavior, which is governed by our genes. Since human genetics have not changed, price action patterns have remained consistent for over 100 years and will remain consistent. The charts from the 1930s Great Depression look identical to the charts from the 1940s WWII era, and they look identical to charts today.

**Key implication**: Crashes are common, not anomalies. They occur on all timeframes (1-min, 5-min, daily, weekly). The trader must expect and be prepared for crashes rather than being surprised by them.

**Anti-Pattern**: Most trading education teaches candlestick patterns as standalone signals ("Dark Cloud Cover = sell", "Shooting Star = reversal"). This is misleading. Successful professional traders do not trade individual candlestick patterns in isolation. They trade trends, channels, prior highs and lows. The candlestick pattern only matters within the context of the market cycle.

## Market Cycle: The Never-Ending Framework

The market operates in a never-ending, continuous cycle of three phases:

```
Trend (Breakout → Channel) → Trading Range → Trend (Breakout in either direction)
```

### The Cycle Structure

1. **Trend** consists of two sub-phases:
   - **Breakout / Spike**: Strong directional bars, small tails, consecutive movement. This is the strongest phase.
   - **Channel**: Weaker directional movement contained between two parallel lines. Channels inevitably flatten and broaden.

2. **Trading Range**: The transition phase between trends. It is not merely a pause — it is a structural bridge. When direction is lost, the market enters a trading range until a new breakout establishes a new trend.

3. **Cycle restarts**: After a trading range, a breakout occurs (either direction), initiating a new trend.

### What the Market Can Do (Exhaustive)

| State | Sub-types | Characteristics |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| **Trend** | Strong (Breakout/Spike) | Big bars, small tails, consecutive, little overlap |
| **Trend** | Weak (Channel) | Bars with tails, overlap, contained between two lines |
| **Trading Range** | Pullback (small TR) | < 20 bars, part of trend, breakout likely in trend direction |
| **Trading Range** | Big TR | ≥ 20 bars, direction lost, breakout can be either way |

### Critical Distinction: Pullback vs Trading Range

- **Pullback**: A small trading range (pause) within a trend. It is part of the trend. Typically ≤ 20 bars long. The breakout is almost always in the direction of the trend.
- **Trading Range**: When a pullback grows so large (≥ 20 bars) that the prior trend is too far back, it becomes a full trading range. Direction is now uncertain.
- **Fractal nature**: All pullbacks are trading ranges on a smaller timeframe. All trading ranges are pullbacks on a higher timeframe.

### The Essential Question: Trend or Trading Range?

The most important fact about price action is determining whether the market is trending or in a trading range. If you are uncertain, it is a trading range. Always answer this question first.

## Pressure: Who is Dominating?

Markets are moved by institutional money. Your job is to identify which side (bulls or bears) is applying the dominant pressure, and trade in that direction.

### Bar Anatomy for Pressure Analysis

Every bar provides pressure information:

- **Close near the high** = bulls were aggressive through the bar's entire life
- **Close near the low** = bears were aggressive through the bar's entire life
- **Long tail above** = bears sold aggressively at higher prices; bulls failed to sustain the high
- **Long tail below** = bulls bought aggressively at lower prices; bears failed to sustain the low

### The Body-Tail Rule

The relationship between body and tails tells you who is in control:
- **Big body + small tails** = one side dominated (Trend Bar)
- **Small body + big tails** = both sides fought, neither won decisively (Trading Range Bar)

### Context Overrides Appearance

A bar that looks like a trading range bar (small body) can behave like a trend bar if it's in a strong trend. A bar that looks like a trend bar (big body) can behave like a trading range bar if the market is sideways. Always prioritize context over individual bar appearance.

### Tails Are Failed Breakouts

Every tail was once a strong trend body. A long tail above means: "price was moving up strongly, then suddenly bears reversed it." Every tail represents a failed breakout attempt. This is why tails are such powerful signals — they represent the failure of one side to maintain control.

## Always In: The Directional Compass

### The Core Question

At every moment, on every timeframe, ask yourself: **"If I was forced to enter the market right now, would I go long or short?"** Your answer is the **Always In direction**.

Key properties:
- The choice is almost always clear
- You can almost always make a choice
- If genuinely unclear, the market is in a trading range

### When You Know the Always In Direction

1. Enter the market in that direction **immediately** or on a **small pullback**
2. If your stop must be wide, **trade smaller size**
3. Institutions enter relentlessly at the market — to make money, you must copy what institutions do

### Beginner Mistake: Waiting for Pullbacks

Beginners wait for pullbacks because they want smaller stops (less risk). They grow anxious when no pullback comes, and they miss huge trends because they're afraid of the large loss a wide stop would cause. Institutions do not wait — they enter at market relentlessly. The beginner's approach of always waiting for a pullback is the opposite of what profitable institutions do.

## Trend Identification Rules

### Bull Trend
- Series of higher highs and higher lows
- Contained in a rising channel
- 80% of reversal attempts will fail
- Swing traders place protective stops below the most recent higher low
- If price falls below a prior low, the trend might be over

### Bear Trend
- Series of lower highs and lower lows
- Contained in a falling channel
- 80% of reversal attempts will fail
- Swing traders place protective stops above the most recent lower high
- If price rallies above a prior high, the trend might be over

### The 80% Reversal Failure Rule
When the market is trending, approximately 80% of attempts to reverse will fail. This means most apparent reversal signals — even strong-looking candlestick patterns — will turn into bull flags (in bull trends) or bear flags (in bear trends) rather than genuine reversals.

### Confusion = Trading Range
If you see BOTH good buy patterns AND good sell patterns simultaneously, the market is in a trading range. Confusion is the hallmark of a trading range. When confused, you have your answer: it is a trading range.

## Trend Bar Classification

Every bar is either a Trend Bar or a Trading Range Bar. Every Trend Bar is simultaneously:
- A **Breakout** (Spike) — initiating directional movement
- A **Gap** — one of: Breakaway Gap, Measuring Gap, or Exhaustion Gap
- A potential **Climax** — if it occurs at the end of a trend

### Gap Types
1. **Breakaway Gap**: Starts a new trend, breaks away from a trading range
2. **Measuring Gap**: Occurs mid-trend, used to project the "measured move" target
3. **Exhaustion Gap**: Occurs near trend end, signals potential reversal

## The 60-40 Rule

The market has inertia (trend persistence). This creates the **60-40 Rule**:
- There is a 60% chance the market will continue to go in the direction that it has been going
- There is a 40% chance of a reversal
- Over many trades, the 60% edge is what generates consistent profitability

The 60-40 Rule explains why fading trends (betting against them) is inherently lower probability than following them.

## Trading Strategy by Market State

| Market State | Strategy | Entry | Exit Management |
|-------------|---------|-------|-----------------|
| **Strong Bull Trend** | Buy only. Sell nothing. | Market, close of any bar, above prior bar | Swing part, scalp part with tight stop |
| **Strong Bear Trend** | Sell only. Buy nothing. | Market, close of any bar, below prior bar | Swing part, scalp part with tight stop |
| **Trading Range** | Buy Low, Sell High | Near range boundaries | Scalp: take profits quickly |
| **Bull Channel** | Buy pullbacks to trend line | Near channel bottom | Target channel top |
| **Bear Channel** | Sell rallies to trend line | Near channel top | Target channel bottom |

## The Truth About Reversal Patterns

**Most reversal patterns fail.** In a strong bull trend, a bearish engulfing pattern at the top is actually a **BUY signal** — because the pattern will likely fail and become a bull flag. The key insight: do not fade a strong trend based on candlestick patterns alone. Wait for the market cycle to clearly transition from Trend → Trading Range → potential Reversal.

**Apple (AAPL) Daily example**: A bearish engulfing pattern appeared at the top of a strong bull trend. Instead of reversing, price continued higher. The pattern became a bull flag, not a reversal. The correct trade was to buy, not sell.
