# Macro-Driven Trade Idea Methodology

Methodology fused from professional investment frameworks; all text is an original paraphrase.

---

## The Macro-to-Micro Translation Framework

Macro analysis without trade ideas is academic. Trade ideas without macro context are gambling. This framework bridges the gap between the macroeconomic dashboard and the stock-level trade pipeline through a structured three-tier cascade:

```
Macro Regime → Sector Preference → Stock Selection
```

Each tier narrows the universe and increases specificity. The cascade is not mechanical — it requires analytical judgment at each transition point. The macro regime tells you where to look; sectors tell you what to look at; stocks tell you what to trade.

### The Consensus Disconnect at the Macro Level

The "variant view" or "consensus disconnect" concept — central to all trading strategies — applies at the macro level as follows: the market is pricing one economic regime, but the leading indicators suggest a different regime is emerging. The disconnect between what the market expects and what the data suggests is the macro edge.

All trading strategies, from merger arbitrage to deep value, share this core question: "What does the market believe, and what do I believe differently?" At the macro level, the answer determines whether the entire portfolio tilts long, short, or neutral.

## Regime-to-Sector Mapping Matrix

### Regime 1: Expansion

**Characteristics**: GDP growth above trend and accelerating, ISM Manufacturing PMI above 50 and rising, employment growing, consumer confidence elevated, credit spreads tightening.

**Favored Sectors**:
- **Technology**: Enterprise and consumer spending rising; upgrade cycles accelerating
- **Consumer Discretionary**: Wage growth → discretionary spending → retail, travel, leisure
- **Industrials**: Capacity utilization rising; capital expenditure cycle expanding; order backlogs building
- **Financials**: Loan growth accelerating; credit quality improving; M&A and IPO activity robust

**Transmission Mechanism**: Rising demand → revenue growth acceleration → operating leverage → margin expansion → upward earnings revisions → multiple expansion (the market prices the growth trajectory, not just the level)

**Avoided Sectors** (defensive rotation out):
- Utilities: Demand inelastic but no growth kicker; relative underperformance as capital rotates to cyclicals
- Consumer Staples: Same dynamic — stable but unexciting in a growth regime

### Regime 2: Contraction

**Characteristics**: GDP growth below trend and decelerating, ISM PMI below 50 and falling, employment softening, consumer confidence declining, credit spreads widening.

**Favored Sectors**:
- **Utilities**: Regulated returns, inelastic demand, dividend yield provides total return floor
- **Consumer Staples**: Non-discretionary demand; margin stability; pricing power for essential goods
- **Healthcare**: Demand inelasticity; regardless of economic conditions, people need healthcare

**Transmission Mechanism**: Demand inelasticity → earnings stability relative to the broader market → relative multiple premium as investors pay up for visibility → defensive outperformance

**Avoided Sectors**:
- Technology: Enterprise spending cycles contract; consumer electronics purchases deferred
- Consumer Discretionary: The first category cut from household budgets
- Energy: Demand destruction from slowing economic activity

### Regime 3: Stagflation

**Characteristics**: Below-trend growth with above-trend inflation, ISM PMI declining but prices-paid component elevated, real wage growth negative, central bank constrained (cannot ease into inflation).

**Favored Sectors**:
- **Energy**: Commodity price inflation directly flows to revenue; capital discipline in the sector limits supply response
- **Materials**: Input cost pass-through; mining and chemical companies benefit from commodity price trends
- **Commodity Producers**: Real asset exposure hedges against currency debasement
- **Healthcare**: Demand inelasticity plus pricing power

**Transmission Mechanism**: Input cost inflation → nominal revenue growth → the ability to pass through costs determines relative winners → companies with pricing power and real assets outperform those with fixed-cost structures and inventory exposure

**Avoided Sectors**:
- Consumer Discretionary: Margin squeeze from input cost inflation + demand weakness from real wage decline combines to create the most hostile environment for discretionary spending
- Real Estate: Rate sensitivity (cap rate expansion as rates rise) + economic weakness = double headwind

### Regime 4: Recovery

**Characteristics**: GDP growth bottoming and beginning to accelerate, ISM PMI crossing above 50, leading indicators turning positive, credit spreads beginning to tighten, central bank accommodative.

**Favored Sectors**:
- **Financials**: Yield curve steepening (central bank holds short rates low while long rates rise on growth expectations) → net interest margin expansion; loan loss provisions decline as credit outlook improves
- **Industrials**: Capacity utilization rising from depressed levels; restocking cycle beginning; operating leverage maximal at the cycle trough
- **Consumer Discretionary**: Pent-up demand releasing; consumer confidence recovering; employment beginning to improve

**Transmission Mechanism**: Recovery is the most powerful regime for equity returns because it combines improving fundamentals (earnings revisions turning positive) with still-depressed valuations (multiples have not yet expanded to mid-cycle levels). The combination of earnings upgrades AND multiple expansion produces the strongest equity returns of any regime.

**Avoided Sectors**:
- Defensive sectors that outperformed during the contraction — the rotation out of defensives is often sharp as capital floods into early-cycle names. Holding Utilities or Staples into a recovery means underperforming the strongest equity rally phase.

## Leading Indicators → Sector Preference Translation

### ISM Manufacturing PMI

The ISM Manufacturing Report on Business is the single most important monthly macro indicator for equity sector rotation. It is timely (released on the first business day of the month), forward-looking (purchasing managers have visibility into their order books), and sector-disaggregated (sub-index data by industry).

**Interpretation framework**:
- **Headline PMI**: Above 50 and rising → favoring cyclicals. Above 50 but falling → growth peaking, begin rotating toward quality. Below 50 and falling → favoring defensives. Below 50 but rising → recovery emerging, begin rotating toward early cyclicals.
- **New Orders sub-index**: Leads the headline PMI by 1-3 months. The most important sub-component for forward-looking sector positioning.
- **Prices Paid sub-index**: Indicator of input cost pressure. Rising Prices Paid with falling New Orders = stagflation signal.
- **Supplier Deliveries**: Rising = supply chain stress (inflationary, favors commodity producers). Falling = slack (disinflationary, favors margins).
- **Customer Inventories**: Too low → restocking cycle ahead (favor Industrials). Too high → destocking ahead (avoid Manufacturing).

### ISM Sector Drilling Methodology

The ISM report includes industry-level data. The professional process:

1. Note the overall ISM direction and level each month
2. Drill into individual industry rankings: which industries are reporting accelerating activity? Which are decelerating?
3. Cross-reference industry rankings with the Prices Paid sub-index — industries with rising activity AND rising input costs have pricing power; industries with falling activity AND rising costs face margin compression
4. Identify divergence trades: Industry A benefiting from ISM tailwind vs. Industry B facing ISM headwind → pair trade opportunity
5. Apply the quantitative screening process ONLY to stocks within the 2-3 most favored and 2-3 most disfavored industries
6. Historical validation: has this ISM signal preceded equity outperformance in this industry before?

**The sector drilling edge**: Most market participants look at the headline ISM number. Far fewer drill into the sub-indices. Almost none apply systematic stock-level screening to the sector-level signal. This is where the institutional edge is generated.

### Yield Curve (10-Year / 2-Year Spread)

- **Steepening (> 150bps)**: Expansion regime. Favor Financials (NIM expansion from borrowing short and lending long), Consumer Discretionary, Industrials.
- **Flattening (50-150bps)**: Late-cycle. Favor Growth/Tech (lower discount rate benefits long-duration assets). Reduce Financials exposure.
- **Flat to Inverted (< 50bps)**: Recession signal with 6-18 month lead time. Defensive posture: favor Utilities, Staples, Healthcare. Reduce all cyclical exposure.
- **Re-steepening from inverted**: Recovery signal. Rotate aggressively into early cyclicals (Financials, Industrials, Consumer Discretionary). This is historically the most powerful equity rally phase.

### Credit Spreads (High Yield OAS)

- **Widening > 400bps**: Credit stress broadening beyond the weakest credits. Contagion risk. Defensive posture regardless of other indicators.
- **300-400bps**: Elevated but not crisis-level. Sector and stock selection matters more than macro positioning.
- **< 300bps**: Benign credit conditions. Risk-on positioning appropriate if other indicators confirm.

### US Dollar (DXY)

- **Strengthening DXY (> 5% over 3 months)**: Headwind for commodities (priced in dollars), emerging markets (dollar-denominated debt burden increases), and US multinationals (FX translation headwind on ex-US revenue).
- **Weakening DXY (> 5% over 3 months)**: Tailwind for commodities, emerging markets, US exporters, and multinationals with significant ex-US revenue (reported revenue and earnings increase when translated back to weaker dollars).

## Inter-Market Confirmation

No single indicator triggers a macro-driven trade. Three markets must tell a consistent story before macro conviction is sufficient to deploy capital:

### The Three-Market Check

**Bond Market** (yield curve shape, credit spread direction, real rates):
- What is the bond market saying about growth expectations?
- What is the bond market saying about inflation expectations?
- What is the bond market saying about credit risk appetite?

**Currency Market** (DXY direction, commodity currency performance, EM FX):
- What is the currency market saying about relative US economic strength?
- What is the currency market saying about global demand (commodity currencies as a proxy for global industrial activity)?
- What is the currency market saying about risk appetite (EM FX as a proxy for carry trade activity)?

**Commodity Market** (copper/gold ratio, oil price trend, industrial metals complex):
- What is the commodity market saying about industrial activity (copper = global industrial demand)?
- What is the commodity market saying about risk appetite (copper/gold ratio rising = risk-on; falling = risk-off)?

### The Confirmation Rule

If all three markets point in the same direction (e.g., bonds say expansion, currencies say risk-on, commodities say industrial demand rising) → high macro conviction. Deploy standard position sizes.

If two markets point one way and one diverges → medium macro conviction. Reduce macro-driven position sizing by 25-33%. Investigate the divergence — is the divergent market providing an early warning of a regime shift, or is it being driven by idiosyncratic factors?

If all three diverge → low macro conviction. The macro picture is unclear. Reduce or eliminate macro-driven positions. Wait for resolution.

### Macro Signal Lead Time
Macroeconomic signals lead equity market moves by 4-8 weeks. The ISM reports what purchasing managers are seeing NOW; equity analysts revise estimates 4-8 weeks LATER. This gap is the window for macro-driven positioning. Enter when the macro signal changes; exit if the macro signal reverses before the equity move materializes.

## ADR International Framework

### ADR Universe Character

With limited exceptions (primarily in pharmaceuticals and biotechnology), the American Depositary Receipt universe is mostly cyclical in nature and mostly old-economy stocks. The dominant sectors represented are: banking and financial services, industrial metals and mining, technology hardware and equipment, travel and leisure, and shipping.

This sector composition has implications for macro-driven idea generation: ADR ideas are inherently cyclical and macro-sensitive. They amplify macro views — if the macro view is correct, ADR positions will produce outsized returns relative to US domestic cyclicals; if the macro view is wrong, the downside is similarly amplified.

### ADR Screening Criteria

**Minimum thresholds for institutional-quality ADRs**:
- Average Daily Trading Value (ADTV) ≥ $10 million — sufficient for institutional execution without excessive market impact
- Market capitalization ≥ $500 million — liquidity and analyst coverage
- SEC filing availability (Form 20-F for annual reports) — information parity with US-listed companies
- Options availability preferred but not required

**ADR universe to avoid**:
- Chinese ADRs in software, computer services, and technology — elevated accounting and corporate governance risk
- Micro-cap shipping ADRs (market cap < $100M) — insufficient liquidity, concentrated ownership, minimal float
- ADRs without recent 20-F filings — information asymmetry; you are trading blind

### Country Selection Framework

1. Identify countries/regions benefiting from the current macro regime (e.g., commodity-exporting countries in expansion, safe-haven currencies in contraction)
2. Screen the ADR universe for liquid names in target countries
3. Cross-validate with LOCAL macro conditions — do not assume regional homogeneity. Brazil and Chile are both in Latin America but have fundamentally different economic drivers (commodities vs. copper/industrial metals). Germany and Italy share a currency but have fundamentally different fiscal positions and industrial structures.
4. Consider the currency overlay: ADR total return in USD = local equity return in local currency + currency movement against the dollar.
   - If DXY is strengthening: the currency component is a headwind. Either hedge the FX exposure or prefer US-domestic ideas.
   - If DXY is weakening: the currency component is a tailwind. Unhedged ADR positions benefit from both the equity move AND the FX move.

### Sector-Specific ADR Warnings

**Pharmaceuticals and Biotechnology**:
The most difficult sector for generating an analytical edge, even for professional traders. Hedge funds employ teams of PhD biochemists who model drug pipelines with probability-adjusted DCF analysis through FDA Phase I/II/III trials. Valuation requires specialized scientific knowledge combined with financial modeling expertise. Avoid this sector in the ADR universe without specialized expertise.

**European Banks**:
Many European banks — particularly in the Eurozone periphery — operate under persistent structural headwinds from decades of declining net interest margins in a low/negative rate environment. Some are effectively "zombie banks" — adequately capitalized on a regulatory basis but unable to generate returns above their cost of capital. When screening European bank ADRs, split the universe into investment banks, retail banks, and hybrid universal banks. Comparisons across these categories are inherently apples-to-oranges.

**International Shipping**:
Greek and other international shipping ADRs tend to be very low market capitalization. The larger-cap names can provide selective long opportunities during expansion periods when global trade volumes are rising, but the universe is small and idiosyncratic. Treat each shipping ADR as a special situation rather than a sector trade.

## Macro Risk Management

### Invalidation Thresholds

Define specific indicator thresholds that would invalidate the macro thesis BEFORE entering any position. The time to decide what would prove you wrong is before capital is at risk, not after.

Example invalidation framework:
- "If ISM Manufacturing PMI drops below 48 and stays there for two consecutive months, the expansion thesis is invalidated. Exit all cyclical long positions."
- "If the 10Y-2Y spread inverts and credit spreads (HY OAS) widen above 500bps simultaneously, the risk-on thesis is invalidated. Exit all risk-on positions and move to defensive."
- "If DXY strengthens by more than 5% in a single month, the dollar-weakness thesis is invalidated. Exit commodity and EM long positions."

### Post-Event Validation

After a scheduled macro event (ISM release, Fed decision, employment report), validate:
- Did the data come in as expected? If yes, hold; the thesis is intact.
- Did the data diverge from expectation? If yes, reassess: is the divergence noise (one month of data) or signal (second consecutive month of divergence)?
- The second consecutive data point that contradicts the thesis is the exit signal. One month can be noise; two months is a trend.

### Correlation Risk

Macro-driven trade ideas are inherently correlated. When the macro view is correct, all macro-driven positions work simultaneously. When it is wrong, all positions fail simultaneously. Position sizing must account for this correlation.

**Rules**:
- Total macro-driven exposure (all positions where the primary thesis driver is macro) ≤ 40% of portfolio gross exposure
- No single sector > 25% of portfolio
- Macro-driven shorts must have individual stop levels, not just a portfolio-level macro invalidation trigger. If the macro thesis is correct but a specific company announces a buyback or receives an upgrade, that individual position can still go against you.

### Contingency Exit Protocol

If the macro thesis invalidates: exit ALL macro-driven positions simultaneously. Do not treat them as independent positions to be evaluated one at a time. The common driver has failed; the common exit is required. Hesitation — exiting some positions but "giving more time" to others — is the most common macro trading mistake.
