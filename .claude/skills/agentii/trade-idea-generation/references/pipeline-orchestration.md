Methodology fused from professional investment frameworks; all text is an original paraphrase.

## The Idea Pipeline Philosophy

### The Knowledge/Process Distinction

Professional trading desks organize work into two fundamentally different categories. This distinction is not taught in academic finance but is universally practiced at the institutional level:

**Fixed Knowledge** — learned once, applied forever:
- Yield curve mechanics and interpretation
- Valuation framework structure (P/E, EV/EBITDA, DCF)
- What economic indicators measure and their transmission mechanisms
- How financial statements interrelate
- Risk management principles and position sizing formulas

**Continuous Processes** — executed on a fixed cadence:
- Weekly yield curve analysis
- Monthly ISM and macro indicator interpretation with sector drilling
- Quarterly screening runs and portfolio rebalancing
- Ongoing catalyst monitoring and position management
- Template completion and trade idea inventory maintenance

The distinction matters because it governs how effort is allocated. Fixed knowledge requires deep upfront investment but no ongoing maintenance. Continuous processes require discipline and consistency — they are the daily work of the professional trader.

### The Idea Quality Imperative

Over a career, two archetypes emerge:

- **Trader A**: Generates abundant high-quality ideas but manages risk only adequately. Over thousands of trades, the quality of the ideas drives cumulative P&L higher. A few exceptional ideas produce asymmetric upside.
- **Trader B**: Manages risk flawlessly but generates few ideas of limited quality. Risk management is necessary but not sufficient — without the fuel of quality ideas, the portfolio trends toward zero over time.

The institutional insight: risk management can be learned in 3-6 months and becomes mechanical thereafter. Once the risk framework is fixed, **idea quality is the only variable that determines long-term P&L**. Therefore, 80-90% of ongoing analytical effort should be directed at fundamentals and trade idea generation.

### The Trade Idea vs. Investment Idea Hard Gate

The single most important distinction in the 20-60 day trading framework:

- A **Trade Idea** has a specific, dateable catalyst expected within 20-60 days. Presenting a trade idea means answering the question: "When does the stock move? When do I get paid?"
- An **Investment Idea** may be fundamentally correct but lacks near-term catalysts. It may be a compelling long-term thesis, but for the active trader it is inventory that cannot be deployed.

Presenting an investment idea as a trade idea is a professional error. The response from any experienced portfolio manager will be: "This is an interesting company. When does the stock move?" If you cannot answer that question with specificity, you have found an investment, not a trade.

## The Trade Idea Generation Equation

```
Trade Idea = Fundamentals + Timing + Trade Structure (Catalysts)
```

### Component 1: Fundamentals
The analytical foundation. Quantitative screening identifies valuation anomalies. Financial statement validation confirms or refutes the signal. Qualitative analysis assesses management credibility, competitive position, and industry dynamics. Without fundamentals, the trader has no basis for direction or conviction.

**Gating rule**: If quantitative and qualitative analysis do not converge on a clear directional view, the idea fails at the fundamentals gate. Do not proceed to timing or structure.

### Component 2: Timing
A fundamentally correct thesis is worthless without a catalyst to trigger the repricing within the trading window. Timing is assessed through catalyst identification (specific events within 20-60 days) and technical/price action confirmation (is the market's price behavior aligned with the fundamental thesis?).

**Gating rule**: No identifiable catalyst within 60 days = no trade. The idea moves to the watchlist.

### Component 3: Trade Structure
How the idea is expressed: outright stock position, options structure, or pair trade. Trade structure can elevate a 7/10 idea to an 8/10 outcome by improving risk/reward characteristics — converting a moderate edge into an attractive payout profile through options, or hedging out market risk through a pair trade.

**Gating rule**: If no trade structure produces an acceptable risk/reward profile (> 2:1 reward-to-risk), do not enter. Not every good idea is a good trade.

## The Pipeline Funnel: Six Gates

Candidates flow through a structured funnel. Each gate has explicit pass criteria and stop criteria. A candidate that fails any gate is either discarded or moved to the watchlist for later reassessment.

```
Universe (S&P 500 + Russell 1000 liquid mid-caps, ~1,500 stocks)
  │
  ├─ Gate 1: Quantitative Screen ──────────────────────────────
  │  Pass: Top/bottom decile by sector-relative forward P/E
  │  Stop: Within normal range (20th-80th percentile)
  │  Output: Ranked outlier list (typically 5-20 candidates)
  │
  ├─ Gate 2: Financial Validation ────────────────────────────
  │  Pass: Revenue, earnings, margin, cash flow all confirm signal
  │  Stop: Financial statements contradict valuation signal
  │  Output: Validated candidates with data quality flags
  │
  ├─ Gate 3: Qualitative Filter ──────────────────────────────
  │  Pass: MOP credible, KPIs improving, management aligned,
  │        catalysts identified within 60 days
  │  Stop: Management credibility failure, no catalysts,
  │        industry headwinds, governance red flags
  │  Output: Qualitatively vetted candidates with conviction scores
  │
  ├─ Gate 4: Template Assembly ───────────────────────────────
  │  Pass: Price target derived, scenarios constructed,
  │        thesis written, risk/reward calculated
  │  Stop: Insufficient data for price target,
  │        risk/reward below 2:1 threshold
  │  Output: Structured trade templates
  │
  ├─ Gate 5: Strategy Match + Historical Analogue ────────────
  │  Pass: Strategy frameworks match, historical analogues
  │        provide precedent, case outcomes support thesis
  │  Stop: No relevant strategy frameworks or analogues found
  │        (proceed with lower conviction, flag gap)
  │  Output: Knowledge-base-validated trade ideas
  │
  └─ Gate 6: Trade Structure + Position Sizing ───────────────
     Pass: Appropriate trade structure selected,
           position size within risk limits
     Stop: No structure produces acceptable risk/reward
     Output: Execution-ready trade ideas in prioritized pipeline
```

### Composite Scoring

Candidates that pass all six gates are ranked by composite score:

```
Composite Score = (Quantitative Strength × 0.30)
                + (Qualitative Conviction × 0.30)
                + (Catalyst Clarity × 0.25)
                + (Macro Alignment × 0.15)
```

Each component scored 1-10. Weighted composite determines pipeline priority. The catalyst weight (25%) reflects its importance as the timing component — even the best fundamental thesis cannot produce returns without a catalyst.

## The Two-Phase Process

### Phase 1: Theory (Fundamentals → Timing)

The analytical work. All desk research, data gathering, model building, and thesis development happens here. No capital is at risk. The output is a completed trade template with price targets, scenarios, and catalyst calendar.

Phase 1 consumes approximately 80% of the total effort for each trade idea. This is intentional — the work done here determines whether the idea deserves capital.

### Phase 2: Implementation (Trade Structure → Risk Management)

The execution work. Trade structure selection, position sizing, entry execution, and ongoing risk monitoring. Capital is at risk. The output is a live position with defined risk parameters.

Phase 2 consumes approximately 20% of effort but 100% of emotional energy. The risk management framework (position sizing, stop levels, portfolio correlation limits) is mechanical and pre-defined — it should not require active decision-making under stress.

## Operational Cadence

### The Two-Ideas-Per-Week Target

Professional output: generate 2 fresh, template-complete trade ideas per week. Maintain 10-15 ideas in active inventory. Not all ideas in inventory will be actionable at any given time — catalysts may be weeks away, timing may not yet be favorable — but the inventory ensures the trader always has alternatives when current positions reach their targets or hit their stops.

### Weekly Work Structure (8-12 Hours)

**Weekend (3-4 hours)**:
- Review the prior week: major news, market moves, position P&L
- Macro review: has the macro view changed? Review leading indicators, yield curve, credit spreads
- Preview the upcoming week: scheduled macro events, earnings reports, economic data releases
- Diarise events that could affect positions and trade ideas

**Monday/Tuesday (3-4 hours)**:
- Review live positions: any stops triggered? Any catalysts materialized?
- Generate 1-2 new trade ideas through the quantitative screening process
- Complete quant and qual work within 2 hours per idea (forced discipline)

**Wednesday/Thursday (3-4 hours)**:
- Continue idea generation and template completion
- Review inventory: which ideas are closest to being actionable?
- Monitor catalyst calendar for upcoming events

### What NOT to Do
Do not sit and stare at the brokerage screen watching P&L tick-by-tick. Monitoring real-time price movements on open positions adds zero analytical value and consumes attention that should be directed at idea generation. Check positions at scheduled intervals, not continuously.

## The 7-out-of-10 Principle

A 7-out-of-10 idea executed consistently, week after week, produces superior long-term results to sporadically chasing 10-out-of-10 perfection. The marginal improvement from 7 to 8 requires 20+ additional hours of research — and the 8 may never materialize into a trade.

Trade structure selection can elevate a 7/10 fundamental idea into an 8/10 risk/reward profile. An options structure that converts a 15% expected return into a 3:1 payout ratio, or a pair trade that hedges out market risk, transforms a good idea into an excellent trade without additional research.

The goal is not perfection. The goal is consistent, repeatable process that produces a steady stream of risk-adjusted trade ideas.

## The Three-Month Competency Timeline

Developing proficiency in the complete pipeline — from macro review through screening through template completion — requires approximately three months of consistent practice. The timeline is consistent across traders:
- Month 1: Learning the tools and templates. Ideas will be slow to generate and of inconsistent quality.
- Month 2: Process becoming familiar. Template completion accelerates. Quality improves as pattern recognition develops.
- Month 3: Approaching unconscious competence. The process flows naturally. The analytical questions become automatic.

The key to accelerating through this timeline is volume: the more templates completed, the faster the pattern recognition develops. Two ideas per week × 12 weeks = 24 completed templates by the end of month 3. This volume is sufficient to develop baseline competency.

## Self-Sustainability Principle

Professional traders find answers themselves. No one provides a KPI encyclopedia for every sector. No one tells you which multiples matter for which business models. No one flags the data errors in your download. The ability to independently research, discover, and verify is what separates professionals from amateurs.

When encountering an unfamiliar sector: read the 10-Ks of the top 5 companies, identify recurring metrics in management commentary, cross-reference with sell-side research, build your own KPI framework. Google what you don't understand. The information is publicly available — the edge comes from doing the work that others won't.
