# Growth Frameworks — Institutional Methodology

Methodology synthesized from institutional investment research; all text is an original paraphrase.

## Builder 6 Traits Framework

Companies that sustain long-term value creation share six observable traits:

1. **Customer Obsession**: The organization is organized around solving customer problems, not around internal functions. Customer feedback drives product decisions.

2. **Contrarian Thinking**: Willingness to pursue opportunities that consensus dismisses. The best investments are often found where conventional wisdom is wrong.

3. **Grit / Long-Term Orientation**: Ability to persist through setbacks. Long-term orientation means accepting short-term costs for long-term gains.

4. **Disciplined Capital Allocation**: Capital goes to the highest-return opportunities regardless of precedent. Zero-based allocation mindset.

5. **Long-Term Orientation**: Measuring success in decades, not quarters. Compensation and incentive structures aligned with long-term value creation.

6. **Capital Markets Knowledge**: Understanding how the company is valued and communicating strategy effectively to investors.

## Synthesized VC T-Framework (5-Firm Consensus)

Multiple venture capital firms use T-based evaluation frameworks. The synthesized consensus across firms identifies five core dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | Key Questions |
|-----------|:---:|------|
| **Team** | 30-50% | Founder-market fit? Complementary skills? Prior success? |
| **TAM** (Total Addressable Market) | High | Large enough for 50-100x return? Expanding beyond initial beachhead? |
| **Technology / Product** | High | 10x improvement (not 10%)? Defensible IP? Network effects? |
| **Traction** | High | Early-stage: paid pilots, engagement. Growth-stage: revenue growth, retention, unit economics. |
| **Timing** | Medium | Why NOW? Market readiness, regulatory inflection, technology maturity? |

### When to Apply VC-Style vs. Traditional Metrics

- **Pre-revenue / pre-profit**: Use T-Framework. Financial metrics are not yet meaningful.
- **High-growth with negative earnings**: Use T-Framework supplemented by unit economics and revenue quality analysis.
- **Profitable, established growth**: Use traditional ROIC/earnings-based frameworks. The T-Framework becomes secondary.

### Corporate Demographics Context

The rate of new business formation and business dissolution varies by industry and economic cycle. Periods of high business formation (births) are associated with increased innovation and future productivity growth. Periods of high dissolution (deaths) clear out unproductive capacity and free resources for more efficient uses.

Innovation case patterns (synthesized from institutional research):
- **Sustained Innovation**: P&G, L'Oréal — consistent R&D reinvestment across decades
- **Turnaround Innovation**: Microsoft (Nadella-era), Oracle (OCI cloud pivot) — cultural transformation enabling new growth
- **Platform Innovation**: Keyence, RELX, Experian — data and network effects compounding over time
